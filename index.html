Why Lightbox exists

It began with a Lightroom renewal: around $150 for one more year of editing photos that were already taken. Every year, that same money could go to the things that actually make photographs: a fast prime, a weekend somewhere with good light, a few prints worth framing.

The free alternatives were capable, but heavy going for a simple routine: shoot, pick the keepers, make them look right, share them. Lightbox is built for exactly that routine, and to feel good doing it.

It isn't a professional suite, and that's deliberate. No catalogue to maintain, no tethering, no print module, no wall of panels. Just the essentials, done with care: light and colour, crop and straighten, masks, copy and paste, a quick export. Plenty for enthusiasts, travellers and weekend shooters, and light enough to stay out of the way.

Free forever: no subscription, no account, no watermark, no limit on how many photos you edit or export.

Lightbox is made by one person. If it earns a place on your Mac, you can support it.

Questions

Which cameras does it open?

Lightbox develops RAW files with the RAW engine built into macOS, so it opens what Apple Photos opens: most cameras from Canon, Fujifilm, Leica, Nikon, Olympus/OM System, Panasonic, Pentax/Ricoh, Sony and more, plus DNG. Apple publishes the full list on its support site (“Digital camera RAW formats supported by macOS”), and new cameras arrive with macOS updates. JPEG, HEIC, PNG and TIFF open too.

Does it change my photos?

Never. Originals are only read. Each edit is a small recipe saved in a hidden .lightbox folder beside the photos, along with your favourites, so edits travel with the folder to another drive or Mac. Exports are always new files.

Which Macs does it run on?

Macs with Apple silicon (M1 or later) on macOS 13 Ventura or later. 16 GB of memory is recommended for smooth editing of 24-megapixel RAW files; Intel Macs aren't supported.

Why does macOS ask before the first launch?

Lightbox isn't sold through the App Store, and notarising it with Apple requires a paid membership, so macOS asks you to confirm it once. Move Lightbox to your Applications folder and open it, then go to System Settings › Privacy & Security and click Open Anyway. On macOS 13 and 14, right-clicking Lightbox and choosing Open also works. After that it opens like any other app, and updates install without asking again. (Keep it in Applications: macOS doesn't let apps update themselves while they run from Downloads.)

How do updates work?

Lightbox checks for a new version about once a day and offers it in a small window with what's changed; you choose when to install. Every update is signed, and Lightbox refuses any download that doesn't carry the right signature. Lightbox › Check for Updates… checks right away, and Settings can turn automatic checks off.

Can it replace Lightroom?

For many hobbyists, yes: it covers what most people use day to day, with faster, calmer editing. If your work depends on catalogues across many drives, tethered shooting, print layouts or plugins, Lightbox isn't meant for that, and that's by design.

Is it really free? What's the catch?

No catch. No trial, account, watermark or export limit, and no plan to add any. Support is welcome and entirely optional.

What does it collect?

Your photos never leave your Mac. Lightbox sends anonymous counts (that it was opened, and how many photos an export contained) so it's possible to know whether anyone uses it, and you can turn that off in Settings. The privacy page lists exactly what is sent.

Something went wrong. How do I report it?

In Lightbox, choose Help › Report a Problem…. It opens an email with your Lightbox, macOS and Mac details already filled in; add what happened and, if you can, the steps that lead to it. If Lightbox quit unexpectedly, Help › Show Crash Reports opens the folder with the report worth attaching. Or write to karate.seating4j@icloud.com.

How do I uninstall it?

Drag Lightbox from Applications to the Trash. To remove everything else, also delete ~/Library/Caches/Lightbox (thumbnails and previews) and ~/Library/Preferences/app.lightbox.mac.plist (settings). Your edits stay in the hidden .lightbox folders beside your photos until you delete them (press ⇧⌘. in Finder to show hidden folders).
